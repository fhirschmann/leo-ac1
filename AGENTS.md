## Working rules

- Talk to the user in German. README, AGENTS.md, code comments, viewer labels, slicer plate/material names and check messages are English.
- `analyze.py fins` was added to the skill on 2026-09-18 and back-tested against `git show HEAD:stl/*.stl` of cce1b82: it reports exactly the four defects that were fixed that day (3 × 12.2 mm saddle webs at x 166.90–168.25, and the 0.83 × 2.3 × 4.0 mm lip crescent at x 214.72) and nothing on the housing. Keep that as the regression case for the check.
- The project follows the skill `openscad-print-project` (`~/.claude/skills/openscad-print-project`). After every model change run the full loop: `print_tools.py export` → `analyze.py islands/overhangs/inlays/thickness` → `slice_check.py` → update README numbers → `build_viewer.py` and republish the viewer artifact → commit.
- `scripts/` must stay identical to the skill (`python3 ~/.claude/skills/openscad-print-project/scripts/skill_sync.py status -C .`). Improve tools in the skill and adopt/install them; no project-local forks.
- No painted or scripted local supports. Design geometry so every part prints without supports (45° flanks, short ledges); the user relies on Bambu Studio's own supports if ever needed.
- No vendor CAD in the viewer, renders or repo: the fan is the simple `fan_visual()` placeholder (user, 2026-09-17; Noctua's CAD terms allow integration and renderings, not passing the model on, and the viewer embeds meshes). A public viewer on GitHub Pages is planned later.
- Viewer artifact (republish from `build/viewer.html`, keep the URL): a private Claude viewer artifact
- README images (`img/`, `VIEWS`) are public: transparent renders for dark pages, every body coloured, no image of the dedication (`body(dedication = false)` in views that see the inside of the front plate). Two-column image table in the README.
- After rendering README images, set each image link's `?v=` query to the first 12 hex digits of that PNG's SHA-256. Forgejo's branch image URLs can retain older cached PNGs even after a page reload.
- Commits: English message, one change per commit, ending with the co-author line; push to Forgejo right after every commit (user), no force push without an explicit request.
- Forgejo remote: `the user's private Forgejo remote`.

## User constraints and preferences

- Toy for the user's son: drop-resistant (walls ≥ 3.2 mm, corner radius ≥ 5), grille openings ≤ 6 mm, no sharp edges.
- Screws only ISO 7380 button head Torx from the user's set: M3 × 6/8/10/12/16/25; M3 × 32 for the fan is bought separately (30 gave only 3.00 mm of thread, the 1 × d floor; the window is 30–33); the bail pivots use the user's M4 shoulder screws (head Ø 8.8 × 3, shoulder Ø 5 × 12, thread 8; 11 on the leoino) with flanged brass bushings (Ø 7 / 5 × 10, flange Ø 10 × 1) as on the leoino case. No countersunk screws.
- Ruthex inserts per datasheet (RX series 08/2022): M3x5.7 hole 4.0, depth ≥ L + 1, wall ≥ 1.6, ≥ 3 mm material to visible faces; M5x9.5 hole 6.4, wall ≥ 2.6. Inserts must be pressable from an accessible side.
- Printer Bambu Lab H2S with AMS, Bambu PETG HF white/grey (plenty in stock), TPU for the feet. Housing white, grille/cover/bail/knob grey.

## Open items

- Accepted residual risk (user, 2026-09-18): the front end of each bail side step is an open wedge that the upper leg sweeps shut from 11.2 mm to 0 as the bail is raised, i.e. through the finger-entrapment band that hinged toy parts over 250 g are meant to avoid (below 5 or above 12 mm). Measured on the meshes: 450.7 mm³ swept per side, mouth 11.20 mm at the top face, 6.10 at z 170, 3.40 lower, and the closing direction is the normal carrying movement, not the folding back. The user accepts it as with any folding handle on a case or toolbox and knows the mechanism from the leoino case. Do not redesign it without asking. If it ever has to go: the only fix that keeps the gap out of the band is moving the stop into the eye pocket so the step front stays solid and the gap by the leg is a constant 0.5 mm — extending the leg forward does not work, it would hit the ramp at a smaller angle and move the carrying angle. The ramp is housing geometry, so it means reprinting the body.
- Fan screws: `len_fan` is 32 (user, 2026-09-18), which gives 5.00 mm of thread and 2.00 mm of tip margin. The engagement reduces to `len_fan - fan_t - 2 * fan_pad`, so it depends on nothing but the screw length and on `fan_t` / `fan_pad`, which come from Noctua's figures rather than a measurement — measure the fan's real thickness over its pads and re-run the export before trusting the number.
- Charge module retention: the only thing holding it is 126 mm² of 1.1 mm VHB on two printed pads plus the 2 mm ledge, in a bay whose only opening larger than 1.6 mm is the grille (which the fan blocks). Charging with the fan off is the documented mode, so measure the steady-state board temperature with the back cover on before trusting tape alone; a printed clip or a tie over the board would be the cheap insurance.

- Measure: battery cable exit; PWM underside parts; switch body depth behind the panel; USB-C shell height/centring with a plugged cable; tape thickness; heatsinks if added; the LED body and flange diameters (its pocket is Ø 3.2 and cannot grow — the flange seats on the ring between bore and boss face and sets the insertion depth, so Ø 3.5 left a 0.15 mm ring and the `led@body` contact check fell to 0.085 mm³; ream the bore if the LED is tight); the fan's real thickness over its pads, which is what sets the screw engagement.
- Fit test (user wants the whole right section, as little material as possible): `test_right` and `test_right_back` (quantity 0) are body and back cover from `part_x` to the right wall and from `shelf_z` up (user: only the upper part, battery holder and USB-C already confirmed), cut with `slice_box()` in print orientation; `TEST_PLATES` puts them with a knob into `stl/leo_ac1_fit_tests.3mf`, sliced with `TEST_PROCESS` (2 walls, 3 top/bottom, 10 % infill) and single-colour from filament 1 (`TEST_FILAMENT`, user; about 89 g, 2.5 h). Check: PWM board flat on its pads, potentiometer pocket, nut from outside, knob over washer and nut, LED, inserts (the power switch is below the shelf now, not in this test). The earlier small slices were removed.
- Fit test of the right section printed (user, 2026-09-17): PWM board lies flat on its pads, nut and washer tighten with enough thread, knob fits and covers them, back-cover piece with lip, bosses and hold-down plate fits. The potentiometer only sat straight with a notch for its anti-rotation tab (slot now in the model, not yet printed). Not tested: LED (not at hand), heat-set inserts (the user saves them; the same Ruthex holes worked on the leoino case). USB-C mechanical fit confirmed 2026-09-16; full cable insertion and fit with soldered wires still open.
- Bail: pinch check of the 0.5 mm gaps between arm, step wall and back cover on the print. M4 inserts: the user uses Ruthex everywhere (2026-09-17), the leoino hole Ø 5.6 × 8.1 works with them.
- Wiring: the NF-A14 PWM has a 200 mm cable plus a 300 mm extension (Noctua; the 400 mm figure was the old fan); stow the excess, keep wires out of the fan, saddles and back lip; leave slack at the back-cover modules.
- The PWM controller draws 0.00 W when switched off (user, 2026-09-17; where it was measured is not stated). Still open: idle draw of the charge/boost module itself, which decides the switch position (battery line: no drain, but charging only with the switch on; O+: charging while off, but the boost converter idles). Check charge-module temperature in the closed housing, also charging with the fan off.
- Unverified electrics (README marks them as listing claims or estimates): charging while running, 2.6 V cut-off, lower charge current by a resistor change (no board documentation saved; do not suggest the modification before the schematic is confirmed), runtime (NF-A14 1.19 W typical / 1.56 W max, converter losses unmeasured). Not yet tested at all: drop and pinch safety, airflow, bushing retention and axial play on the print.
- Air duct vs. real fan frame: the duct check uses the fan envelope; the real Noctua frame has chamfers, some back-flow is possible (a foam ring could help).

## Verification and known limits

- `print_tools.py export` checks meshes, bed placement, 210 assembly pairs (4 allowed bail-swing pairs skipped), alignment of round features from the CSG dumps (179 coaxial pairs, none 0.2–2 mm off axis), contacts, stops, clearances (knob ≥ 0.4 running clearance, charge module ≥ 5 mm from the fan), 11 removal paths, 17 insert probes, screw engagement, colour pieces and project checks in `print_project.py` (knob, bail, feet, dedication line gaps). Reports: `docs/verification.json`, `docs/slicer-summary.json`.
- Blind spot found on 2026-09-18 (user spotted the symptom on the model): neither `thickness` nor `ridges` sees a free-standing thin tower. `thickness` passes anything at or above `--min-width` (1.2), so the 1.35 mm saddle webs went through, and `ridges` only sections at z 0.4. The new `fins` command scans every layer for components whose smaller in-plane extent is thin and reports how far they run unbraced. On the current parts it leaves only intended features: the 3 mm lip, the 2.4 mm partition, the 1.6 mm webs between intake slots, the 1.5 mm legs of the cable-tie loops (bridged, 2.5 mm tall) and the asserted 2.2 mm web between cable passage and pin notch in the left PWM rib.
- `analyze.py thickness` finds one wall under 1.2 mm, intended: the 0.8 mm skin in front of the LED. Until 2026-09-16 the CLI passed `--min-span` as the sharp-edge angle (2 degrees), which hid thin curved walls; fixed in the skill, re-run found nothing new. With `--min-span 0` the knob and bail report only edge and chamfer tips. The potentiometer wall under washer and nut is 1.8 mm; the switch-well wall 1.56 mm across its 45° flanks.
- Physical feedback (2026-09-16): the user has the printed parts and reports that the USB-C socket fits perfectly. No geometry adjustment is needed for this fit. The exact printed set and full cable insertion were not specified. The PWM board stood crooked because the full-width ribs pressed on its solder pins → pads under the pin-free long edges, 0.3 mm lower. The outside counterbore left the potentiometer wall too thin → housing pocket from inside, washer and nut on the flat outer face. The battery holder fits (foam tape).
- Bought parts are envelopes; physical fits confirmed so far (on the 120 mm prints): USB-C socket, battery holder, PWM pads, nut and knob, back-cover fit-test piece; the changed 140 mm parts need their own checks. The fan envelope includes the NF-A14 pads (141 × 141 × 27 mm). Strength, airflow, thermal performance and the remaining fits have not been validated.
- Accepted small overhangs: groove ends, screw-head pocket rings, knob flutes, connector openings, switch-floor ledge, PWM back pads (3.5 mm over the pin notch). Historical (120 mm state, not reproduced by the current default run): `analyze.py overhangs test_right_back` reported the exact-45° switch-well flanks (about 120 mm² per layer) although the same flanks in `back` stay below the threshold: raster artifact of the shifted slice, not a real overhang. Likewise `overhangs bail` reports the exact-45° bed chamfer along the right lower leg only (about 165 mm², the mirrored left leg stays below the threshold).
- Project 3MFs made before 2026-09-18 opened in Bambu Studio with the default print (2 walls, 5/3 shells, grid; housing about 300 g instead of 391 g): the GUI resets every value not named in `different_settings_to_system`, which the CLI left empty. `slice_check.py` now fills it; after opening, the housing should show 5 walls and about 400 g.
- Slicer layout (user, 2026-09-18): housing and back cover sit in the middle of plates 1 and 2 with the prime tower behind them (`CENTRE_PLATES`), and `infill_direction` 0 for every part so the first-layer lines on the front face run parallel to its long edge (default 45 degrees gave diagonal lines). Checked in the plate G-code: 99 % (housing) and 98 % (back cover) of the bottom-surface length at 0 degrees.
- Freshly exported CGAL STLs (body, cover) are not byte-identical to committed ones; compare geometry, re-slice after export.
